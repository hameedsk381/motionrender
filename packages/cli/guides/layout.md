# layout: let the content decide the page

A video about a topic must not come out as headline, cards and a button. Decide in this order, and write the layout fresh each time.

1. **Find the one picture that tells the story and make it the page.**

| The content is | The page is |
|---|---|
| a cost, rate or score changing over time | a line or bars that fill the frame, the story pinned to the points, a headline above and the final number below |
| a process or a journey | a path with named stops that a marker travels along |
| two options, or before and after | two halves, each its own colour, filling the frame |
| one figure that matters | the figure as giant type on a band that runs to both edges |
| a history | a line you travel along, each event pinned where it happened |
| a share of a whole | one ring or a stack of blocks, labelled directly, no legend |

2. **Take the palette and type from the subject.** News: cream, ink and one red with a serif. Security: black and phosphor with mono. Climate: deep green and gold. Finance: deep blue and one warm accent. A brand: its own colours. One accent, one display face, one body face.
3. **Set the pace from the beats of the story**, not a fixed scene count. A short idea is ten seconds, not thirty.
4. **Check it does not look like your last video.** If the page structure repeats, start the layout again before you touch a colour.

`mr explainer outline "<what the video explains>"` proposes which scene kinds a subject calls for, in order, for the explainer route.
